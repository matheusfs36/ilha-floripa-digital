# Partner Brief — Ilha Floripa Digital

> A short document to place in the hands of potential collaborators, community groups, researchers, innovation programs and local partners.

## What is the project?

Ilha Floripa Digital is an open public-facing R&D project rebuilding Florianópolis, Brazil, as a **living, navigable and progressively playable digital territory**.

The project combines real geography, 3D environments, procedural generation, WebGPU / Unreal Engine, multimodal AI, world memory and community participation.

Its longer-term hypothesis is simple:

> **What if the real city could continuously help build its own digital world?**

Residents, visitors and players may eventually contribute photos, videos, audio, scans, stories and local knowledge. AI can help interpret and organize those inputs, but publication into the world requires staging, review, rights checks and governance.

## Why this is different from a conventional 3D map

We are researching the intersection of:

- game / living-world systems;
- digital twin and geospatial reconstruction;
- cinematic storytelling;
- community co-creation;
- cultural and territorial memory;
- multimodal AI;
- local portfolios and discovery;
- environmental and coastal simulation.

The real territory is both **subject and participant**.

## Example experience

A painting of Praia do Moçambique can be associated with the approximate physical viewpoint from which it was painted. Inside the digital world, a player can find that point, align the painting with the reconstructed landscape and discover the artist, story and place through the experience itself.

This same mechanic can be applied to photography, historical images, architecture and cinema.

## What participation can look like

A contributor does not need to know game development.

Useful participation can include:

- documenting a place with a phone;
- recording a local story with permission;
- validating a reconstructed building or path;
- contributing environmental or historical knowledge;
- helping design community methodologies;
- testing accessible capture protocols;
- providing technical expertise in GIS, 3D, AI, WebGPU, Unreal, photogrammetry or ocean/coastal science;
- connecting creators, neighborhoods, institutions and local initiatives.

## Minimum equipment

For many contributions, the minimum is simply:

- a modern smartphone;
- camera and video;
- location services;
- original files and basic contextual notes.

More advanced work can optionally use 360 cameras, LiDAR, photogrammetry, dedicated audio, drones operated under applicable rules, GNSS/RTK and professional imaging systems.

See [FIELD-CAPTURE-GUIDE.md](FIELD-CAPTURE-GUIDE.md).

## What we want from early partners

At this stage we are especially interested in partners who can help us test **methods**, not only technology.

Good early conversations include:

1. How can residents participate without requiring specialist training?
2. How should contributions be validated and credited?
3. How do we create safe consent and rights workflows?
4. How can local creators and businesses appear without turning the world into advertising?
5. How can community participation become a repeatable field methodology?
6. How can the project create value for neighborhoods while also improving the game/world?
7. Which small pilot in Florianópolis would generate the most learning?

## Suggested first pilot

A compact pilot can focus on **one place and one community loop** rather than the whole island.

Example structure:

```text
choose a small area
→ define capture protocol
→ invite a small contributor group
→ collect photos / video / audio / stories
→ AI-assisted organization
→ human validation
→ integrate one visible world improvement
→ create one cinematic interaction
→ measure participation and quality
```

A pilot around one beach, street, cultural venue or neighborhood can test the full social and technical loop before scaling.

## What already exists

The project is already being developed in public with a repository, public site and playable/browser experiments. A larger private R&D laboratory is kept separate so public collaboration can remain understandable and safe.

The public repository intentionally records stable concepts, architecture notes, experiments and contribution protocols without exposing credentials, restricted assets or unstable internal automation.

## A useful first conversation

A first meeting does not need to solve the project.

The ideal outcome is to identify **one small, testable collaboration** with:

- a place;
- a participant group;
- a capture method;
- a validation method;
- one visible result inside the digital world;
- clear learning criteria.

That gives both sides something concrete to evaluate before expanding the relationship.
